# Project review — 2026-08-06

Written after the 3-year backfill completed. Covers what the review measured,
including **two measurements that were wrong and had to be retracted**, and the
one direction decision that came out of it.

## Why the retractions are recorded rather than deleted

The wrong measurements are kept here on purpose. Both looked alarming, both were
plausible, and both would have justified urgent work that was not needed. A
later session re-deriving them from the same data would reach the same wrong
conclusion unless the correction is written down.

---

## The news-loss question, measured three times

CLAUDE.md singles out news as the one unrecoverable source: RSS holds a rolling
buffer with no history, so an hour not collected is permanently absent. So "are
we losing articles?" is the highest-stakes question in the repository.

### Measurement 1 — WRONG

Compared `article_id` sets between consecutive snapshots. 92.9% of
feed-transitions shared no articles at all, which read as total buffer turnover.

**Why it is wrong:** `fetch()` drops already-seen articles before writing
(`kr_news.py:568-570`), so each file holds **only new** articles. Zero overlap
between consecutive files is what a working de-duplicator produces. The
measurement was evidence that dedup functions, read as evidence of loss.

### Measurement 2 — BIASED, unusable

Compared, per feed, the oldest *new* article in each snapshot against the newest
previously stored one. Produced "478 feed-hours lost over a 66-hour window",
with 매일경제 at 72%.

**Why it is unusable:** for a slow feed, consecutive articles are naturally more
than 30 minutes apart, so a normal publication gap is indistinguishable from a
hole. The number measures how often these outlets publish, not what was missed.

### Measurement 3 — the collector's own signal, trusted

`check_feed_continuity` (`kr_news.py:175`) already answers this correctly: it
compares each feed's **buffer oldest** — the oldest item the feed is still
serving, before dedup — against the newest article stored. If the buffer starts
*after* the last thing stored, the window moved past everything known and the
difference is genuinely gone.

Run live at 2026-08-06T05:09Z, 1.5 hours after the last stored snapshot:

```
12 of 13 feeds overlap stored history — margins of 17 to 68 hours
etnews_economy alone had rolled 5.3 hours past
```

**Conclusion: loss is real but confined to one feed.** Feed buffers are far
deeper than the original plan assumed — it budgeted for a 50–120 item rolling
window; `infostock_all` was holding 67.9 hours. That margin is what has been
absorbing an irregular collection cadence.

## The finding that survived

Not the volume of loss — the fact that **nobody is told about it**. CLAUDE.md
requires:

> Missing data appears in the report header, not only in logs.

`check_feed_continuity` detects the `etnews_economy` hole correctly and reports
it into GitHub Actions logs, which nothing reads. The 5.3-hour hole above was
found by running the check by hand during this review, not by the system raising
it. That is a silent failure, which this project defines as its worst outcome.

Compounding it: the workflow fires roughly **10 times a day against a cron that
declares 31**.

```
declared  "17,47 0-6 * * *" + "17 7-23 * * *"        = 31 runs/day
observed  10 · 10 · 10 over 2026-08-03..05
times     0337, 0613, 0910, 0958 …  unrelated to :17/:47 — 20–40 min late
```

GitHub documents scheduled workflows as subject to delay and drop under load.
The deep buffers currently absorb this, but every missed run spends margin.

**This closes when step 10 renders a header, not before.** It is the strongest
single argument for the ordering decision below.

## Two smaller defects, both measured

**Day-scoped lookback blinds the continuity check overnight.** Both
`seen_ids(root, collected_at.date())` and `newest_stored_per_feed(root, ...)`
are scoped to the current date. On the first run of a day there is nothing to
compare against, so the check no-ops — precisely across the overnight gap, which
is the longest one. The same scoping re-stores the previous day's buffer:
**761 of 5,998 stored rows (12.7%) are cross-day duplicates**, 28% on
2026-08-04.

**`macro.py:244` will become an error.** `pd.Timedelta(days=1)` added to a
Timestamp raises a NumPy generic-unit DeprecationWarning — 9,350 occurrences in
one test run, on pandas 2.3.3 / numpy 2.5.1. `next_tradeable_open` sits on the
path of every collector, so this fails widely rather than locally when NumPy
follows through.

## Direction: step 10 before steps 6–8

SPEC §12 orders the embedding pipeline, golden set and bake-off before the
report renderer. **The renderer should come first.**

The argument is PREREGISTRATION, which splits the two-week validation into two
groups with different prerequisites:

| Preregistered metric | Needs |
|---|---|
| Pipeline integrity, data consistency, entity accuracy, whether the briefing is read | **steps 10–12** |
| Inter-model agreement, golden-set performance, reproducibility, measured cost | steps 6–8 |

Building 6–8 first leaves the two-week clock unstarted. Building 10–12 starts
half the preregistered metrics immediately *and* closes the silent-failure
finding above, because the header is where missing data is required to surface.

**What it costs.** The briefing ships without news commentary (SPEC §2.2⑧) and
without `news_polarity`, holding rating coverage at 0.75 of 1.10. That is above
`min_weight_coverage: 0.5`, so ratings are real — the same reasoning that put
step 9 ahead of 6–8, unchanged and still valid.

## Not findings

- **`us_price.py` is not dead code.** `us_price_alpaca.py:91` imports its
  `SCHEMA`, `KNOWN_VALUE` and `MISSING_THRESHOLDS`; keeping Tiingo as a
  cross-check is a recorded decision, and it is what caught the IEX discrepancy.
- **`valuation_band` computing to NaN for every row is not a bug.** It needs 756
  sessions against 728 available. It is one of Ricky's parked decisions
  (MANUAL-TASKS §7), not a defect.

## Agreed plan

**Step 0 — clear the ground before building.**

1. Fix `macro.py:244` so the deprecation cannot become a failure.
2. Widen the news lookback past the day boundary — closes the overnight blind
   spot and the 12.7% duplication with one change.
3. Correct the documentation drift the completed backfill left behind.

**Then step 10**, the renderer, with the report header carrying missing-data,
feed-continuity, `weight_coverage` and ambiguous-ratio lines. **Then 11–12**,
delivery and schedule, which starts the PREREGISTRATION clock.

`rev_4w` and the four-year backfill stay parked; neither blocks the renderer.
