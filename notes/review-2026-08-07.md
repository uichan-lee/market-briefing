## Findings

### H1 — The rating archive double-counts revisions and contains a phantom session

`load_rating_history()` (`src/report/render.py:1029`) concatenates every parquet
under `data/ratings/` with no per-session selection. Measured today:

```
2026-08-03    31 rows / 31 tickers
2026-08-06   124 rows / 31 tickers    ← counted four times
2026-08-07    31 rows / 31 tickers    ← coverage 0.000, every score exactly 0
                                        the session had not opened
total        186 rows for 62 real ones
```

Two separate defects meeting:

1. **`write_ratings()` archives every re-render as `-vN`** and deliberately so —
   "what was published is a fact about that run." That stance is defensible and
   matches the `data/raw/` discipline. The reader is what is wrong: nothing
   picks one version per session, so a session re-rendered four times during
   yesterday's debugging carries four times the weight of one rendered once.

2. **`write_ratings()` runs before `render()`** in `main()`, so the
   zero-coverage guard added on 2026-08-06 — which correctly refuses to *publish*
   a rating table when every ticker is at 0% coverage — never prevented the same
   frame from being *persisted*. `data/ratings/2026-08-07.parquet` is the direct
   product of that gap: 31 rows of the all-관망 bug, sitting in the evaluation
   dataset for a session that had not opened.

**Why this is the top finding.** It does not bite today — §7 only reads
`date.nunique()`, so the visible symptom is a report claiming three sessions of
history when two are real. It bites at PREREGISTRATION §8.4, where the composite
score becomes an IC computation. A quadruple-weighted session and a session of
31 zeros are exactly the kind of silent corruption the project's determinism
discipline exists to prevent, and they will be hardest to notice at the moment
they matter most.

**Fix, and its cost.** Two changes, neither destructive:

- `write_ratings()` refuses a frame whose every row is at zero coverage — the
  same condition `render_ratings` already refuses to publish. Cost: none; the
  condition already means "no rating was possible."
- `load_rating_history()` selects the newest version per session. Cost: a real
  decision, not a free one. The newest is the *corrected* rating, but the
  *published* rating was the first — and for evaluating what the system actually
  told Ricky to think, the first is arguably the honest one. Recommend newest,
  because a re-render happens when the earlier run was wrong, and preserving a
  known-wrong rating for evaluation measures the bug rather than the method.
  Either way the choice belongs in PREREGISTRATION §R, since it changes what the
  evaluation reads.

The existing `2026-08-07.parquet` is Ricky's call: deleting it is permitted
(`data/ratings/` is derived output, not raw collection) but it is also the only
record that the bug reached publication.

### H2 — Two weights are live for features that do not exist

`config/rating.yaml` carries `news_polarity: 0.20` and `rev_4w: 0.15` while
`src/features/compute.py:58` produces neither. `rate()` renormalizes over present
features, so:

```
ceiling      0.75 / 1.10 = 68.2%
floor        min_weight_coverage = 50%
margin       18.2 points, not 50
```

The file's own comment (lines 34–38) says uncommenting a weight for a feature
that never arrives "would be actively harmful, not merely useless" for exactly
this reason. The two weights predate that comment and were never commented back
out — so the config violates a rule it states about itself.

The consequence is not theoretical. Losing `foreign_flow_5d` alone drops a
ticker to 40.9% and forces 관망; losing any two mid-weight features does the
same. `short_ratio` is *routinely* absent — KRX publishes short balance on a T+2
lag — which puts the normal recent-session state at 59%, nine points above the
floor. The float-boundary bug found on 2026-08-06, where a ticker at exactly
0.55/1.10 was demoted, was this margin being thin enough to hit.

**Fix, and its cost.** Comment both weights out until the features exist, per the
file's own rule. Cost: the header line `등급 근거 충족도: 0.75/1.10 (68%)` is
currently doing real work — it tells Ricky the rating rests on five of seven
intended inputs. Commenting the weights out makes coverage read 100% and the
rating look better-supported than it is. So the fix is only correct if paired
with an explicit header line naming the *inactive* features, alongside the
existing `미구현 섹션` line. Do not do one without the other.

### M1 — Standalone news-run failures still reach only the Actions log

Today's fix made `check_feed_continuity` honest about feeds that did not answer,
but for the hourly `collect-news` workflow that honesty still lands in a log
nobody reads. `data/status/` is gitignored, so the JSON bridge that
`scripts/collect_daily.py` writes and `src/report/render.py:983` reads only works
*within* one job.

Partially mitigated by accident: the report run executes `kr_news` itself, and
its continuity check compares the live buffer against all stored history, so
accumulated loss does surface in the briefing header. What does not surface is
loss during hours when no report runs.

Not ranked higher because the loss it hides is now small — see D1.

Harmless, but it is the kind of thing that reads as uncertainty about whether

## Direction

**The architecture is holding, and the deterministic-first constraint is the
reason.** Four days of live running produced four defects — a late cron rating an
unopened session, an email sent as raw markdown, eight hours of news lost to a
runner-allocation failure, and a continuity check silently passing on feeds that
never answered. Every one was caught by a deterministic check or by Ricky reading
the output, and every one was diagnosable from stored evidence. None required
guessing what a model had done. That is the constraint paying for itself.

**D1 — the news-collection worry was largely a single bad feed, and it is gone.**
The alarming number is real: GitHub fires roughly a third of declared runs, and
hourly coverage over 2026-08-03..07 was 42.6% (40 of 94 hours). But coverage is
the wrong metric — what matters is whether a gap exceeded the feeds' buffers.
Measured over 45 observed gaps: median 2.03h, max 8.72h, and with
`etnews_economy` removed **1 gap (2.2%)** exceeded the fastest remaining buffer
of 6.5h. The correct read is that news collection is adequate, not fragile, and
the earlier framing over-weighted one feed whose 30-item buffer spanned 1.2h.

This retires the standing question of whether to move collection off GitHub
Actions. At 2.2% realised loss the answer is no — the alternative costs a host,
a deploy path, and a credential store, to recover a fraction of one feed-hour a
week.

**D2 — the golden set is now the whole critical path, and that is worth stating
plainly.** Steps 6, 7 and 8 are one dependency chain, and every one of them sits
behind 100 hand-labelled articles. `news_polarity` at 0.20 is the largest single
weight in the rating and the only one that requires language understanding —
i.e. the only place the project's LLM budget is justified under CLAUDE.md's
determinism rule. Until it exists, the briefing is a flow-and-price model with a
news *section* rather than a news *signal*.

No pivot is recommended. The sequencing was correct — building the deterministic
path first meant the four live defects were found against a system whose outputs
could be checked. But the remaining work is not parallelisable around Ricky, and
that should be visible rather than implicit.

**D3 — the 2-week gate (step 13) needs its start date pinned.** PREREGISTRATION
§8.5 gates on two weeks of ratings. There are two real sessions of rating history
(2026-08-03 and 2026-08-06). If the clock is meant to start from the first
*scheduled, unattended* run rather than the first manual render, that is
2026-08-06 and the gate lands around 2026-08-20 — but only if H1 is fixed first,
because the gate reads exactly the archive H1 corrupts.
